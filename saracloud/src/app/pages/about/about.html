WHERE IT ALL STARTED

My first real step into tech.

My journey into technology didn't begin with me knowing exactly where I was going. It started with an opportunity to learn something completely different from what I was studying. I joined the She Code Africa Academy, Cohort 3, where I began learning DevOps.

It was a 12-week intensive and hands-on program that combined technical learning with practical assignments, weekly assessments, soft-skill sessions, and a capstone project. Throughout the program, I was introduced to essential DevOps concepts and tools while also developing important professional skills through interactive sessions. The combination of technical training, practical tasks, assessments, and soft-skill development provided a well-rounded learning experience.

The journey was not without its challenges. There were moments when I felt overwhelmed by the complexity of the concepts and the pace of the program. There were times when something worked perfectly in a lesson but refused to work when I tried it myself. Errors would appear, configurations wouldn't behave as expected, and sometimes I had to go back to the beginning just to understand where I had gone wrong.

I had moments where I questioned whether I was actually getting it. But instead of allowing the difficulty to become a stopping point, I started treating each problem as something I needed to understand. And that gradually changed the way I learned. I stopped expecting everything to make sense immediately. I became more comfortable searching, experimenting, making mistakes, breaking things, fixing them, and trying again.

I didn't even have my own laptop when I started. I had to borrow one, and when it began struggling with commands and installations, I had to keep finding ways to learn until I could finally get mine. That moment meant more to me than simply owning a laptop — it meant having the freedom to keep building.

Looking back, She Code Africa Academy was more than the place where I was introduced to DevOps. It was where I began to understand that learning technology would require patience, curiosity, persistence, and a willingness to keep going even when I didn't have all the answers.

And that was only the beginning.

Building on what I started

Taking the next step in tech.

After completing my three-month DevOps training at She Code Africa Academy, I realized that I need to understand more about the other side of what I was learning. I had learned about deployment, infrastructure, automation, and how applications are managed, but I wanted to understand where the application itself begins.

That was what pushed me to apply for the Women Techsters Bootcamp Cohort 5.1. I wanted to learn computer languages and understand how software is developed from the beginning. I was also interested in Artificial Intelligence and wanted to learn how I could use AI to make the process of building and deploying applications faster and more efficient.

For me, it was not about starting a completely different journey. It was about adding another part to the journey I had already started with DevOps. I wanted to understand the application side so that I could have a better picture of what happens from development to deployment.

During the bootcamp, I had the opportunity to put what I was learning into practice. At the end of the program, my team members and I built an educational web application aligned with the United Nation Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Building that project was a major part of my experience because it allowed me to move from learning concepts to actually creating something.

The experience helped me see how software development connects with DevOps and cloud. I began to understand that writing the application is one part of the process, while deploying, automating, managing, and making that application accessible to users is another.

Looking at my journey from She Code Africa Academy to Women Techsters Bootcamp, I can see how the two experiences complement each other. One helped me understand more about DevOps and cloud, while the other helped me understand software development. Together, they have helped me build a stronger understanding of how technology solutions are created and delivered.

This is why I continue to explore both sides as I grow in software development, DevOps, and cloud engineering.

Growing through collaboration

Cloud Build With Peers

My journey in tech continued with Akwannya Hub’s Cloud-Build-With-Peers Cohort 1, where I had the opportunity to learn, build, and grow alongside other aspiring cloud professionals. After gaining a foundation in DevOps and software development, I wanted to keep challenging myself through practical experience and collaboration.

During the cohort, I worked with Team Eta, and being part of the team taught me that growth in tech is not only about what I can achieve individually, but also about what we can accomplish together. We shared ideas, tackled challenges, learned from one another, and worked together on technical tasks and presentations.

The experience also provided opportunities to demonstrate my commitment, technical growth, and ability to collaborate effectively. I was recognized among the cohort’s Most Engaged Participants, finishing in third place for my consistent participation and active contribution throughout the program.

As a member of Team Eta, our collective efforts were recognized across several categories. We placed third as Best Team, achieved second place for Best Technical Presentation, and were also recognized as the third Most Innovative Team. These achievements reflected our ability to collaborate, develop practical solutions, communicate technical concepts effectively, and deliver quality work as a team.

Beyond the recognition, the experience strengthened my understanding of cloud technologies while improving my communication, problem-solving, teamwork, and presentation skills. Working closely with my teammates also taught me how different perspectives can contribute to better technical outcomes.

Akwannya Hub became an important part of my continuous growth in tech. It gave me the opportunity to apply what I was learning in a collaborative environment, contribute meaningfully to a team, and gain practical experience that continues to shape my journey in cloud and technology.
